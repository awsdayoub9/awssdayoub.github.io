## The six phases

A phase closes when its deliverable is in your hands, not when I say it is finished.

### Phase 1 · Discovery

**What happens.** A first call, then a requirements session with your stakeholders, key users and any consultant already advising you. We walk through each process as it runs today, noting where standard Odoo covers it and where it does not. Nothing is priced yet.

**What you receive.** A written record: the processes in scope, the open questions, and a first view of what will be configured and what will need code.

### Phase 2 · Specification

**What happens.** Each requirement is mapped to standard Odoo where it fits; the remainder is documented as a gap with a proposed design, each challenged first, because a small change to a process is often cheaper than a module.

**What you receive.** The functional specification, marked line by line as configured or built; the written scope with its timeline and assumptions; and the quote.

### Phase 3 · Build

**What happens.** Configuration and custom development on a staging database, in short cycles, each ending with a demonstration of working software against the specification. Custom modules extend standard models and views by inheritance; the core is left untouched.

**What you receive.** Staging access, each demonstrated increment, and the custom modules in a repository you own.

### Phase 4 · Migration

**What happens.** Source data is cleansed, then migrated into a test database as a trial. Opening balances, stock levels and open documents are compared with the source, corrections are made and the trial repeated until the comparison is clean; on a version upgrade the same discipline covers every custom module.

**What you receive.** The validated test database, migration notes recording what was checked and how, and any migration scripts written for your project.

### Phase 5 · UAT

**What happens.** Your key users work through their own processes on the migrated test database, against the specification. Findings are logged, fixed and retested until the sign-off list is empty. Training runs alongside, in Arabic or English.

**What you receive.** A sign-off checklist with every item closed, training sessions for key users, and user documentation.

### Phase 6 · Go-live

**What happens.** Cut-over on an agreed date, then hypercare: I stay close to your HR, finance and procurement users through the first weeks and resolve what they raise, as detailed on the [support page](/services/odoo-support/).

**What you receive.** The handover pack: documentation, admin access, and ownership of the repository and the database.

## Terms of engagement

### Configuration before customisation

Standard Odoo wherever it covers the need. Custom code only for a gap recorded in the specification, and only after a change to the process has been considered.

### You own the code, the repository and the data

Custom modules are delivered in a repository you own, and the database is yours; with the specification, code and documentation in your hands, another developer can pick up the work at any time.

### Nothing reaches production without staging and UAT

Every change is built on a test database and signed off by your key users before it is deployed, after go-live as much as before it.

### Written scope before work

Work starts once the scope is written and agreed; the quote follows discovery. If the scope moves, the change is written up, priced and approved before I act on it.

### Documentation and handover, with support that can be agreed

Documentation and training are in the scope from the outset. The handover pack is described under go-live, and a support retainer can be agreed if you want continuity after it.

## Engagement models

Four ways to engage; the quote follows discovery in every case.

- **Fixed scope after discovery.** For implementations and larger builds. Discovery and specification run first; scope and price are fixed together.
- **Support retainer.** For a system already live: a named person for the issues your users raise and small changes, with terms agreed once the system is stable.
- **Hourly customisation.** For well-defined work on a running system, such as a report or an approval workflow. Scoped in writing before I start, billed by time.
- **Migration audit.** For companies on an older version: a review of the database and custom modules, a list of what will break, and a written upgrade plan.

## Decisions that sit with you

The key user per department who signs off, named before discovery; the edition and the hosting, settled before the quote; the cut-over date and who confirms each area, fixed before go-live. Each goes into the scope with its date; a late decision moves the timeline more than any module does.

## What a written scope contains

The scope lists:

- The processes in scope, named the way your business names them.
- Each requirement, marked as configuration or custom development.
- The integrations, with the direction of the data and the system of record for each.
- The data to be migrated, its source, and the checks that will prove the migration.
- The user roles and the access each needs.
- The deliverables per phase and the timeline, with its assumptions.
- The acceptance criteria that UAT will test against.
- What is out of scope, written down so it is not assumed.
- The change process: how a new requirement is written up, priced and approved.
